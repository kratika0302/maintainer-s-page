Maintainer Programs

India's FOSS ecosystem runs on people working unpaid, unseen, and alone.

  • Almost nobody knows they exist. There are hundreds, maybe thousands, of Indian FOSS maintainers doing serious work with no way to be found.
  • Even fewer are supported. We've backed under 50 people in six years. Fewer than 10 projects in our community are financially sustainable today.
  • Demand already outpaces us. Last year we approved fewer than 15% of applications, and still ran a deficit.

Who do we support

The global FOSS ecosystem rests on the shoulders of individuals.

Behind almost every project that powers modern software infrastructure, there is a person or a small group of people who decided to build something and keep it alive.

Who is a maintainer?

We anchor this term not around artefacts, but an individual’s role in the ecosystem.

“A maintainer is anyone who takes ongoing responsibility for a FOSS or digital commons project. That could be writing code, reviewing contributions, managing releases, writing documentation, or stewarding the community around it. The project may be newly created or long-established. We do not define maintenance by the size of a project or the number of users, but the sustained, often unpaid labour that goes into keeping something useful alive.”

You might be who we're looking for if you are:

  • Creators of new FOSS projects
  • Maintainers of existing FOSS projects
  • Maintainers of upstream dependencies and infrastructure
  • Individuals working on translations, localisation, accessibility, etc.
  • Contributors to the digital commons ecosystem (open data, open science, etc.)

Why do we support

Because maintenance is invisible, and invisible work burns people out.

Behind almost every project that powers modern software infrastructure, there is a person, or a small group of people, who decided to build something and then decided to keep it alive. Foundations, companies and communities all matter, but the atomic unit of FOSS is the individual who shows up. We've spent a while trying to find the right word for that person — "developer" is too narrow, "creator" leaves out the people sustaining someone else's work, and "contributor" is too broad. The word that comes closest is maintainer, and we define it deliberately loosely:

Anyone who takes ongoing responsibility for a FOSS or digital commons project — writing code, reviewing contributions, managing releases, writing documentation, or stewarding the community around it. We don't define maintenance by the size of a project or its user count, but by the sustained, often unpaid labour that goes into keeping something useful alive. — FOSS United, Maintainer Program Thesis

That includes people starting new projects, people keeping old ones on life support, people maintaining the upstream dependencies everyone else quietly relies on, and people doing the unglamorous work of translation, accessibility and localisation. None of it shows up on a resume the way a startup does. All of it is infrastructure.

The problem, in three parts

Discovery

There's no reliable way to find adopters, contributors or users for a FOSS project. Creators end up shouting into HackerNews, Reddit, LinkedIn and X, hoping something sticks.

Community

Maintaining a niche project usually means spending most of your time alone behind a screen. There's rarely a peer group of people who understand exactly what you're stuck on.

Sustainability

The hardest problem is money. FOSS work carries a cultural expectation of being free, even as cloud companies build entire businesses repackaging it for profit.

We built the Maintainer Programs to work on all three at once — not as a one-time grant, but as a long-term relationship with a maintainer's journey, from being completely unknown to running something sustainable. It grows out of, and eventually subsumes, our existing grants program. Read the full Maintainer Program Thesis and the Grants Thesis for the complete picture.

How do we support

A maintainer's journey has three layers, not one grant.

This isn't a funnel, and not every maintainer needs the deepest layer. Our job is to keep expanding all three.

Layer 1

Discovery

Visibility into the FOSS community, and an interface with the foundation. This is the front door — open to anyone.

  • Forklore — a public directory of Indian FOSS maintainers
  • fundingjson — publishing a project's funding needs so programs like the FOSS Pledge can find it
  • Campaigns, blog posts and case studies (#MeetTheMaintainers, #ForkloreFridays)
  • Visibility at FOSS United events, including IndiaFOSS
Layer 2

Community

Opt in once you're in the discovery layer. This is the layer we expect most maintainers to actually live in — including graduated grantees.

  • A mailing list and forum space just for maintainers, plus training on security, accessibility and compliance
  • Community calls, and the Maintainer Summit held the day before IndiaFOSS
  • The Maintainer Pack — cloud credits, discounted tools, travel scholarships and workshop access
  • Volunteer support from the foundation on design, community building, marketing and technical guidance
Layer 3

Support

Where the foundation invests real time and money into a small pool of projects — funds, community building, infrastructure and help finding adoption.

  • Grants, fellowships and travel funding, plus help raising money elsewhere — see the grants directory
  • Hands-on help building a community around a project, including through Season of Commits and FOSS Hack
  • Sponsored security, accessibility and compliance audits, plus hosting support
  • Press coverage and introductions to people who could adopt the project

Get involved

Be a part of the change

You're already doing the work. Let us make it easier.

If you're building or sustaining a FOSS or digital-commons project out of India — alone or with a small crew — this program exists for you. Get discovered, find people who understand exactly what you're stuck on, and if the project's ready, get support with no strings attached.

Fund the individuals, not just the infrastructure.

If your company runs on FOSS — and it does — you already depend on maintainers you've never met and probably never paid. Back them directly through the grants program, or put cloud credits, tool licenses, hardware and co-working space straight into their hands through the Maintainer Pack.
